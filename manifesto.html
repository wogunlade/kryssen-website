  1. Content should help a buyer do something.

    Understanding matters, but useful content should also help someone compare, calculate, prepare or act.

  2. The buyer’s decision should determine the format.

    Do not begin with “we need a post.” Begin with what the buyer needs to understand or use.

  3. Useful expertise matters more than constant publishing.

    The advantage is not volume. It is turning real company knowledge into material buyers can trust and use.

  4. Inbound and outbound should work from the same knowledge.

    The answer discovered independently should strengthen the next sales conversation—not contradict it.

  5. Original insight can help create the category.

    Reports, benchmarks and executive education can shape the questions the market learns to ask.

  6. Content becomes infrastructure when someone operates it.

    It needs evidence, ownership, approvals, distribution and a commercial signal—not a publishing calendar alone.
